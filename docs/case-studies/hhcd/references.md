# References: three observations for this page

Playbook §7. Links checked on 10 October 2026.

1. **Put the demo straight after the sentence that explains it, then say what to notice.** In [Building a toast component](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-toast-component), Emil Kowalski places each working demo right after its explanation. A one-line caption follows, telling you what to watch for ("Notice how…").
   - **On this page:** the arrangement switch sits directly under its paragraph. Its caption tells the reader what to do and what to watch. Captions throughout say what the image proves, not what it shows.
2. **Show a decision as a pair, at the same size.** [How we redesigned the Linear UI](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui) puts alternatives side by side (before, concept, after; contrast 30 against 100) and explains the trade-off in the text. Its captions only label the images, which is the part not to copy.
   - **On this page:**
     - The geographical prototypes appear as a pair.
     - The free network and the columns share one frame, so the switch is the comparison.
     - The colour-contrast card shows each brand colour beside its text-safe variant.
3. **Neutral surfaces, colour from the work.** [Granola](https://www.granola.ai/) keeps its pages neutral, with short sentence-case headings and wide product frames on quiet fields. The colour comes from the product shots.
   - **On this page:** the case file stays on the site's paper. The Centre's four type colours appear only in the work itself: screenshots, the type shapes and the contrast specimens. A single muted teal handles links and markers.
