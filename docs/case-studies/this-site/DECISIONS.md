# This site (the shell): decisions for Chongbo

Open points from the shell work of 11 October 2026 (`shell/micro-interactions`). Each is live as described; say the word and it changes.

1. **The PRINT label on phones.** At 390px the engraved word is about 6px tall: present, not readable. The button is large and the lamp turns green, so I left it. Alternative: hide the label under 700px, or show a one-line hint under the printer on phones.
2. **The paper's hint once a card is picked**: "Add a line about the role, or press the red button." It is instructional text, which the copy rules prefer to avoid, but it replaces a cycling example that looked like it would be sent. Alternative wording: "Anything to add?" (shorter, but doesn't point at the button).
3. **Provenance line wording.** Model: "Chosen by Claude from my case files, one section per project. The words are mine." Fallback: "Matched by keyword from my case files, one section per project." It exposes which path answered, in plain words, without naming internals. If you'd rather keep that private, drop the first clause of each.
4. **Footer**: "Claude chooses which sections of my case files to print. It never writes them." Replaces the "keyword matching as a fallback" line. Confirm you want the fallback unmentioned in the footer (it is still stated on a result).
5. **Kraft as the shell's colour** (`--kraft` #d9c59e, same in both themes). Used only for the capsule links. If you'd prefer the pills to follow the theme in dark mode, the token needs a dark value and two more contrast pairs.
6. **Hammer strength.** Letters move ±1.4px and ±6° per strike, ×2.2 on every fifth strike, up to six strikes per hover. Lighter than the reference and than the Nike version you found too much. Tell me if it should be lighter still, or if the fifth-strike "hard" knock should go.
7. **Eyes follow the pointer within 220px of the About pill**, not only when hovered, so they come alive as the pointer approaches. Could be hover-only.
8. **On a case file the Work pill becomes "All work" with the drawn line.** It is the shell's only back link. The HHCD and Nike templates have no back link of their own; if they add one, it should use `<Pill kind="back">` so the behaviour matches (noted in `SHARED-CHANGES.md`).
9. **The "Or browse all work" pill** keeps the "Or": it answers the printer, not the nav. Could be "Browse all work".
10. **Profile summary** (`content/index.ts`): "brand work that has to earn a tap" is the one phrase in the shell that is more clever than clear. Suggested: "and brand work measured by what people tap". Not changed, as content files are yours.
11. **Not done, worth a prototype:** sheets feeding out of the tray before the deck fans; a "Printed for: …" chip row above long decks; a `missing` line that offers the nearest work.
12. **Local builds in this sandbox** stub `src/components/case/fonts.ts` because Google Fonts is unreachable; the stub was never committed. WildEcho still loads Open Sans and Nunito Sans from Google at build time, so any offline build fails there until WildEcho moves to Geist (playbook §7 retires the Wix fonts once a project is rebuilt).

## 12 October, night: the projector (`shell/projector`)

Built from `HANDOFF-projector.md`, the material pack (`Chongbo-Ning-Projector-Material-Pack`, V5 machine) and the two flow references. Live on the branch's Vercel preview only; `main` is untouched. Each point is how it is now; say the word and it changes.

24. **Display type.** Geist everywhere by default (playbook §7: one family for the shell and the case files). Instrument Serif is ready for comparison: add `?type=serif` to the preview URL (headline, card questions, the wall's heading, slide titles). Self-hosted (OFL, `src/app/fonts/`), not preloaded, so Geist visitors never download it. Recommendation: Geist, for one voice across the site; the serif is warmer and reads more like a slide, so it is a real option.
25. **The hand is a stack of index cards**, not a fan: each card's top strip shows its question in one line, the blank card in front (ruled, red caret) takes free text or a pasted job description. From reference 1, panel 2. A fan hid the questions unless you hovered, and doesn't work on touch.
26. **One card at a time.** Picking a card flies it into the magazine (its place in the hand stays, dashed); picking another puts the first back. Writing on the blank card takes a printed card out; the blank card goes in when you press the red button (or Enter).
27. **The machine's status line** is its only instruction: "Insert a card" → "Ready to project" → "Reading your card…" → "Finding the work that answers it…" → "Projecting". Errors stay in the light room on the same line (lamp red): "The lamp needs a minute to cool down…", "The slide jammed…", "The projector can't reach the slides…". The engraved PROJECT under the red button is about 7px at desktop size: decorative, the button's accessible name is "Project".
28. **The dark room.** The machine steps back to the left (on phones: below the slide), the lens lights and a measured beam reaches the slide's near edge (reference 1, panel 3). Every slide in one answer is the same size (the tallest), like a real projector; text slides are centred. Very tall evidence (HHCD's colour specimens) scrolls inside the slide on desktop rather than stretching every slide. Controls on one row: ← 01 / 03 → and a dot per slide (project name on hover and for screen readers), then "Change the card" and "All work". Arrow keys move, Escape brings the lights up, swipe on touch.
29. **Slides carry the case-file evidence** (`sheetEvidence`, the same block as the card and as `/ask`), and a small "● Accessibility" in the corner says which capability the card was chosen for. Tags only list the section's other capabilities.
30. **Wording.** Home note under the machine: "Claude chooses which sections of my case files to project. It never writes them." Footer on other pages: "…to show." (was "…to print"). The wall's heading is the API's `line`; the card is quoted beside it.
31. **Header.** The kraft capsules and the hammer are gone. Work: a small stack of slides whose front one is lifted and dropped back on hover/focus. About: the eyes, as before.
32. **404** is an empty slot in the carousel: a lit slide, "There's no slide in this slot.", Back to the projector / Browse all work.
33. **Open Graph image** for the home page: the headline beside the projector with the accessibility card in the magazine (`src/lib/og-projector.png`).
34. **Material used.** V5 empty-magazine machine (cropped, WebP), the plain paper card (texture for every card), the landscape lit panel (9-slice for any slide size), the warm walls (very low opacity, kept so secondary text passes AA on their darkest pixel). Not used: the five-card machine (one card at a time), selected-card and focus-glow overlays, the oblique and lit renders (old wide slot). A V5 render with the lens lit would let the lens glow be the photograph's own.
35. **Not done, worth a look:** a "Next" link inside the slide (the references have one; the controls row already does it); the light room in dark mode is close to the dark room (the change is mostly the lens and the beam); sound (a slide-change click) was left out.
36. **How the files got here:** the material pack was on your Mac only, so I pushed it from your Mac to a temporary branch `assets/projector-pack`, copied it into this branch and deleted that branch. Vercel may show a failed preview for it; it can be ignored.

## 12 October, morning: the dark room, redone (v6 material)

After Chongbo's review: the small machine at the left of the dark room clashed with the text and looked out of place. Rebuilt from the two flow references and the v6 animation pack (`chongbo-ning-projector-animation-v6`).

37. **We move in on the machine.** Pressing the red button drops the card into the gate; the room goes dark and the machine grows into a close, slightly soft foreground in the bottom-left corner, cropped by the window, fading into the dark away from the lens. The lens lights by cross-fading to the v6 lit render (same framing), and the v6 beam photograph is aimed in code from the lens to whatever is lit. Replaces 28's small machine and drawn cone.
38. **Thinking happens in the dark.** A list of four steps at the top left (reference 1, panel 3): "Reading your card", "Finding the work that answers it", "Choosing the sections to show", "Projecting". The first two follow the real request; the last two follow the answer. Meanwhile the light on the wall is out of focus; when the answer arrives it comes into focus as the story cards. The whole sequence takes at least about 2 s so it reads, longer only if the request does.
39. **Browsing is a row of story cards** (reference 1, panel 4; v6 story panels): "Selected for your card", the answer's line, the card quoted, "3 stories". The card in the light faces us; the others turn away into the dark (v6 motion logic, without wrapping round). Arrows, dots, keyboard, drag or swipe. A card shows its picture (the evidence's first image or the video's poster) or, without one, its first sentence.
40. **Open a card and it becomes the full slide** (reference 1, panel 5): it grows out of the card, with "← Back to the selection", the evidence block, "Open full project →" and "Next story →". Open, it sits in front of everything (the machine goes softer); arrows move between stories, Escape goes back, a second Escape brings the lights up.
41. **The footer waits** while the room is dark; the dark room fills the window and the page doesn't scroll.
42. **What would make it better (material):** a lit render of the same machine at three-quarter view, lens towards the right, for the foreground (the front-on render reads well, but the light leaving a lens that faces us is a stretch); and a dust-in-the-beam loop (short transparent video or a sprite) to give the beam life.
43. **The hand is a file of index cards**, tops aligned (no more hand-placed tilt): every card the same paper with drawn edges, the question set in the card's top band, its number at the right; each top edge throws a little shadow on the card behind. The blank card is a slip like the others ("+ Write your own question") and opens to ruled lines when you write. A card pulled from the file changes from slip to card on its way to the magazine. (Reference 1, panel 2.)
44. **The dark-room machine is sharp** (no blur), and figures, diagrams and comparisons on an open slide take its full width under the text, so the numbers no longer collide.
45. **The ¾ renders** in `docs/design/projector-flow.md` (A–C) are what the dark room is waiting for; until then it uses the front view.
46. **Two cameras in the dark, from the cream series pack.** While it thinks, the machine is the close foreground seen from the side (`machine-34-off/on.webp`, one pair that overlays pixel for pixel, so the lens lights by cross-fading); the front view hands over to it as we move in — the new view comes in over the old one and only then does the old one go, so they never show through each other. When the light comes into focus we cut through the dark to behind the machine (`machine-rear.webp`), looking at the wall with it: the beam (`beam-rear.webp`) leaves from the far end of the lens. Change the card plays it backwards to the front view. Each view's lens is measured on its photograph and the beam is placed from there (`beamPlacement` takes the beam's own geometry).
47. **From behind, only the lit render is used**: the pack's rear on and off renders don't overlay exactly, and the lens faces away, so there is nothing to see light up. It's dimmed and warmed (lit only by the wall), and its light is quieter than while it thinks, because we read by it. The rear render has five cards in its magazine; at this size, in the dark, cut off by the frame, it doesn't read — a single-card rear render would replace it like for like.
48. **Dust only where the light is**: the dust layer drifts in two sizes at different speeds, masked by the beam photograph itself, so specks never float in the dark. Still with reduced motion.
49. **A press is never dropped while a slide moves**: Next, Previous and Escape during an open, change or close wait for it to finish, then act.
50. **Measured, not guessed: why it juddered.** Frame times taken through the whole flow showed the dust (a background moving through a masked, blended layer) repainted the light every frame and halved the frame rate even at rest; the focus pull was an animated blur over the whole row; the dark room's pictures were only fetched at the press. Now the dust is pre-cut to each beam's shape and drifts on transforms in its own unblended layer, the focus pull is opacity and scale with the soft patch fading out under it, the cards out of the light are shaded by a layer's opacity instead of a filter, the beam is placed by one transform (so it glides on the compositor), and the dark room's pictures are fetched and decoded while you choose a card, its camera and light already in the page, unseen.
51. **One camera path into the dark.** The front view and the three-quarter view are drawn into the same moving rectangle, from where the machine stood to where it ends; half-way, at the fastest point, one hands over to the other in a fifth of a second — a cut on the action — so the change of angle reads as the camera going round. The lens lights and the light reaches out (a soft front running along the beam) while the camera is still settling, not after it stops.
52. **A slide opens as a window, not a stretch**: it grows from the card's own rectangle (a clip), so no word is ever squashed; the words come up once there is room. The machine drops away out of the frame as you lean in and the light glides after the lens, into the slide's corner. Closing plays it back.
53. **Keyboard focus on a story card** is a thin light ring round the card, on the wall, rather than a dark box drawn inside it.
54. **Up to three cards.** A slide magazine holds a row of slides, so the projector takes up to three cards: each new one goes in behind the last (a slot further back, higher, a touch smaller), the blank card goes in last when you press, and pressing a card that is in (its empty place in the file says "In the projector · take out") takes it back out while the others move up. A fourth is refused with a shake and the status line says why.
55. **One request per card, not one merged question.** Merging would make the wall's heading answer everything at once and thin out each card's stories. Each card is asked on its own (the home page still sends only `{ brief }`, so nothing in `src/lib/ask` changed); the wall shows the first card's stories, then the next card's after a tick in the dots, and the heading changes to that card's answer ("Card 2 of 3 · No. 04") with a small knock as the magazine advances. A story two cards both bring up is shown once, where it first came up, and its slide says which cards it answers. A card that jams is named; the others are still shown. Unit-tested in `joinReels`.
56. **The no-JS path and `/ask` stay one card**: the form still submits one card's text.
