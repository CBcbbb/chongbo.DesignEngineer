Proposal 01 · Home, at rest

Show work before asking

Today the first screen asks a question and waits. A recruiter who doesn't pick a skill sees a printer and one quiet link. Both options put real work in the first screen; the printer stays the hero.

1440 × 900
390 × 844

What changes

The page as it is on design/printer-v2.

On load, the printer feeds a short slip out of its front slot, in the same stepped motion as a thermal print, once. It lists the four case files with what each shows. Pressing the button still prints to order.

Under "Or browse all work", a slim row of the four projects with their covers. The printer is 580px wide instead of 680px on a 900px-high screen, so the row sits in the first screen. Nothing moves on load.

Why

  • Seeing work is the first thing most recruiters do; the skills cards are the second.
  • Covers are recognisable at a glance; four titles in a list are not.
  • The slip keeps the machine's story: it has already printed something for you.

Switch to A or B.

Cost

Half a day. A CoverRow under .alt, cover images for the two logistics projects (exported from their cover art), one clamp change in printer.css. Hidden once a print arrives, as .alt is now.

A day and a half. The slip's layout, the stepped reveal, reduced motion, where it goes when the visitor prints (it should feed back in), and the phone layout under a smaller machine.

None.

Proposal 02 · Home, after a print

The end of the print, the moment of highest intent

A visitor who has read six sheets has a reason to act, and today the only action is "New request". The three things they want at that point: a way to write to you, your CV, and a link to send a colleague.

1440 wide · after "Design systems" and "Motion"
390 wide

What changes

The deck counter and "New request".

A paper stub, perforated along the top, under the strip: the link to this print with Copy, the email with Copy, and the CV. "New request" moves under it as a quieter button. The same stub ends /ask, so a shared link ends the same way.

A final sheet in the strip, the same size as the card sheets, with the same three actions set large. The counter counts it.

Why

  • Visible without swiping to the end, so it works for a one-sheet print too.
  • The link reuses the topics the printer understood, never the visitor's own words, so a pasted job description stays out of the address.
  • Red stays for the one main action, as on the printer: the CV.
  • Big and calm, but seen only by people who reach the last sheet.
  • On phones it sits after every sheet, the same as A.

Switch to A or B.

Cost

One day. A small client component with two copy buttons (Clipboard API with a select-the-text fallback), the link built from asked, a public/cv.pdf you supply, and a test that the link reopens the same sheets.

None.

Proposal 03 · /work

Visual cards, 2 × 2

Four text-only cards in a 3 + 1 grid leave the fourth project alone on its row and show no work. Covers, a year and a role make the list scannable in a few seconds.

1440 × 900
390 × 844

What changes

Each card: its cover at 16:10, the client, the title, the one-liner, then role and year under a rule. The whole card is the link, with a round arrow that moves on hover. "Full project" goes.

One project per row, the cover on the left at half the width. Larger covers, but only one and a half projects per screen.

The page today.

Why

  • Four is an even number: 2 × 2 never leaves a card alone.
  • Role and year answer the recruiter's first two questions about each project.
  • The covers are the case files' own (two of them are the covers' art on their colour field), so the list and the pages match.

Switch to A or B.

Cost

Half a day, plus a year field in content/schema.ts (a shared file: noted for SHARED-CHANGES.md). HHCD's year is a gap for you to fill; Nike's 2023 comes from the Creative System file's date.

None.

Proposal 06 · Small visual points

Four details

Each one is small; together they decide whether the shell feels finished.

6.1 The "About" eyes read as "00"

Two upright ovals with centred dots look like the digits 00 when nothing moves, and phones never hover, so most people only ever see the digits. Recommended: A, with the idle behaviour on touch screens.

Nowat rest
About
A · almond eyes, looking at the wordRec.
About

Wide eyes with pointed corners can't be read as digits. At rest the pupils sit off-centre, towards "About", so the icon is already looking. Move the pointer here: they follow it, as today.

On touch screensA, plus

No hover, so the eyes live on their own: a blink every 6 to 9 seconds, and a glance towards the last tap. Both are tiny, under 0.3s, and off with reduced motion. Cost: a quarter of a day in Pill.tsx.

Option B, if you'd rather: drop the icon on phones under 420px and keep the word. Simpler, but the header loses its one playful detail where it is seen most.

6.2 The printer against the first screen

The machine's width follows the window's height. On a laptop it uses almost all of the first screen, so nothing below it shows. Proposed: clamp(420px, (100svh - 560px) × 1.9, 580px), which keeps it the largest thing on the page and frees about 50 to 70px for the covers row.

6.4 Fewer full sentences in mono

Mono reads as a label or a number. Rule proposed for the shell: mono for labels, numbers, the printer's paper and short tags; sans for anything that is a sentence. A quarter of a day, all in printer.css and components.css.
